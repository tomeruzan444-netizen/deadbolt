# Research Facts: Deadbolt Nation Locksmiths

Compiled 2026-11-12. Every fact below was read on the page listed. "high" = official publisher, manufacturer, or government page stating it directly. "medium" = reputable secondary source, an older page, a summary page standing in for a paywalled standard, or an official page that may be out of date. Do not add facts that are not in this file. Prices and licensing rules change, so re-check them before publishing.

Paywalled standards (ANSI/BHMA, UL, NFPA, ICC): facts come from the publisher's public summary pages or from manufacturer pages. Each one is marked as such.

## 1. ANSI/BHMA deadbolt grades (A156.40, A156.36, A156.13)

- FACT: A156.40 (Residential Deadbolts) does NOT use Grade 1/2/3. It rates each lock A, B, or C in three separate categories: Durability, Strength and Security, and Finish. The lock must meet every test for its grade in each category on its own. | SOURCE: BHMA, "ANSI/BHMA A156.40 - 2015, Residential Deadbolts" (news page, standard approved Jan 15, 2015) | URL: https://buildershardware.com/News/News-from-BHMA/ansibhma-a15640-residential-deadbolts | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: On BHMA's residential label (the "Certified Secure Home" label), the ratings are C = Good, B = Better, A = Best, given for Security, Durability, and Finish. BHMA published A156.39 (locksets) and A156.40 (deadbolts), its first residential standards, in 2015. | SOURCE: Locksmith Ledger, "BHMA Certified Secure Home Label Raises the Standard for Residential Door Hardware" (July 3, 2017) | URL: https://www.locksmithledger.com/door-hardware/article/12340474/bhma-certified-secure-hometm-label-raises-the-standard-for-residential-door-hardware | CONFIDENCE: medium
- FACT: The current edition is A156.40-2025. BHMA's public summary says Grade A residential deadlatches must pass 400,000 open/close cycles, the force to retract the latch or bolt may not exceed 9 in-lbf of torque, 17 security tests are specified (one puts 1,350 lb of force on the door against the deadbolt), and finish is checked with an ASTM B117 salt-spray test. The summary gives no numbers for Grades B or C. (Public summary only; the standard is paywalled.) | SOURCE: BHMA Hardware Highlights, "A156.40 - 2025 Residential Deadbolts" | URL: https://buildershardware.com/ANSI-BHMA-Standards/Hardware-Highlights/A15640-2025-Residential-Deadbolts | CONFIDENCE: high (summary page)
- FACT: How a manufacturer shows the A156.40 rating: Schlage's B60 single cylinder deadbolt lists "ANSI/BHMA A156.40-2015 Grade AAA in Security, Durability and Finish" and also "ANSI/BHMA A156.36-2010 Grade 1." In other words, "AAA" (residential) and "Grade 1" (auxiliary-lock standard) appear together on one product. | SOURCE: Schlage, Single Cylinder Deadbolt (B60) product page | URL: https://www.schlage.com/en/home/products/B60FFF.html | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: The Grade 1/2/3 scale for deadbolts comes from A156.36 (Auxiliary Locks). The edition listed by BHMA is A156.36-2020. Its scope is auxiliary locks, with operational, cycle, strength, security, and finish tests. BHMA's summary says Grade 1 deadlocks must pass 250,000 cycles, a key-operated lock must open with no more than 9 lbf-in of torque, there are 7 security tests, and one of them applies 1,350 lb of force to the door against the deadbolt for Grade 1. It gives no numbers for Grades 2 or 3. (Public summary only.) | SOURCE: BHMA Hardware Highlights, "A156.36-2020 Auxiliary Locks" | URL: https://buildershardware.com/ANSI-BHMA-Standards/Hardware-Highlights/A15636-2020-Auxiliary-Hardware | CONFIDENCE: high (summary page)
- FACT: A156.13 covers mortise locks and latches. The current edition listed by BHMA is A156.13-2022. BHMA's summary says Grade 1 mortise locks must pass 1,000,000 cycles with a 10 lb load, lever locks must open with no more than 28 in-lbf of torque, and 15 security tests are specified, including a 1,200 in-lbf locked-lever torque test and impact tests. (Public summary only.) | SOURCE: BHMA Hardware Highlights, "A156.13-2022 Mortise Locks" | URL: https://buildershardware.com/ANSI-BHMA-Standards/Hardware-Highlights/A15613-2017-Mortise-Locks | CONFIDENCE: high (summary page)
- FACT: Allegion's cycle-test table (dated 2013): Bored locks (A156.2) 1,000,000 / 400,000 / 200,000 cycles for Grades 1/2/3; Mortise locks (A156.13) 1,000,000 / 800,000 / 800,000. The same document says ANSI/BHMA certification requires third-party testing and that random samples are audited. The table does not cover A156.36 deadbolts. | SOURCE: Allegion, "Understanding BHMA Grading & Testing" (Rev. 12/06/2013) | URL: https://dev-cs-schlage-commercial.allegion.com/content/dam/allegion-us-2/web-files/allegion/information-documents/Understanding_BHMA_Grading_and_Testing_009968.pdf | CONFIDENCE: medium (2013; editions have changed since)
- FACT: BHMA's Certified Products Directory (CPD) lists every product certified to ANSI/BHMA standards. Certification relies on tests verified by independent third-party labs, with periodic recertification. Anyone can search it by standard or by brand. The program name is "BHMA Certified." | SOURCE: BHMA, Certified Products Directory | URL: https://buildershardware.com/Certification-Program/Certified-Products-Directory | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: BHMA's own summary of A156.40 says the CPD lists the cycle, operational, strength, security, and finish test requirements for certified residential products. | SOURCE: BHMA Hardware Highlights, A156.40-2025 | URL: https://buildershardware.com/ANSI-BHMA-Standards/Hardware-Highlights/A15640-2025-Residential-Deadbolts | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: Also listed on BHMA's standards index: A156.2 (2022) Locks and Latches; A156.5 (2020) Cylinders and Input Devices for Locks; A156.30 (2025) High Security Cylinders; A156.28 (2023) Master Keying; A156.39 (2025) Residential Locksets and Latches. | SOURCE: BHMA, Hardware Highlights index | URL: https://buildershardware.com/ANSI-BHMA-Standards/Hardware-Highlights | CONFIDENCE: high

## 4. Egress and double-cylinder deadbolts (codes)

- FACT: IRC R311.2 (as adopted in the 2018 NC Residential Code and reproduced in an official NC interpretation): "Not less than one exterior egress door shall be provided for each dwelling unit. The egress door shall be side-hinged..." with at least 32" clear width and 78" clear height, and "All interior egress doors and a minimum of one exterior egress door shall be readily openable from the side from which egress is to be made without the use of a key or special knowledge or effort." This is NC's adopted wording. Model IRC wording differs slightly by edition. | SOURCE: NC Dept. of Insurance / Office of State Fire Marshal, formal interpretation letter "2018 NC Residential Code Section R311 Means of Egress" (Oct 18, 2023) | URL: https://ncosfm.gov/2023-1018-lavalle-2018-ncrc-r311-means-egress/open | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: The same NC interpretation says only one code-compliant exterior egress door is required. Other exterior doors need not meet the egress-door dimensions, and it draws a distinction "between the required egress door and all other exterior egress doors." | SOURCE: NC OSFM interpretation (Oct 18, 2023) | URL: https://ncosfm.gov/2023-1018-lavalle-2018-ncrc-r311-means-egress/open | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: The model IRC R311.2 wording, as quoted for the 2009 IRC: "Egress doors shall be readily openable from inside the dwelling without the use of a key or special knowledge or effort." | SOURCE: iDigHardware (Lori Greene), "Decoded: Key-Operated Locks" (reader quote of 2009 IRC) | URL: https://idighardware.com/2014/04/decoded-key-operated-locks-june-2014/ | CONFIDENCE: medium (secondary; ICC site blocked automated reading)
- FACT: Adoption varies by state. Minnesota's 2020 Minnesota Residential Code adopts the 2018 IRC with amendments. | SOURCE: Minnesota Dept. of Labor and Industry, "Emergency Escapes and Rescue Openings" fact sheet | URL: https://www.dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/fs-emergency-escapes.pdf | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: IBC general rule (cited as IBC/IFC 1010.1.9 in the 2015/2018 numbering): "egress doors shall be readily openable from the egress side without the use of a key or special knowledge or effort." NFPA 101 7.2.1.5.3: locks "shall not require the use of a key, a tool, or special knowledge or effort for operation from the egress side." Edition years were not stated on the page. | SOURCE: iDigHardware, "FF: Key, Tool, Special Knowledge and Effort" (Oct 2019) | URL: https://idighardware.com/2019/10/ff-key-tool-special-knowledge-and-effort/ | CONFIDENCE: medium
- FACT: IBC 2024 Section 1010.2.4, item 3 (commercial): on the main door(s) of Group A (occupant load 300 or less), B, F, M, and S occupancies and places of religious worship, key-operated locks on the egress side are allowed only if (3.1) the door is the main exterior door or main tenant-space door, (3.2) the lock is readily distinguishable as locked, (3.3) a durable sign on or next to the door reads "THIS DOOR TO REMAIN UNLOCKED WHEN THIS SPACE IS OCCUPIED" in letters 1 inch high on a contrasting background, and (3.4) the building official may revoke this for due cause. In 2021 the same provision was numbered 1010.2.4 as well. | SOURCE: iDigHardware, "Decoded: Key-Operated Locking Devices" (July 2025) | URL: https://idighardware.com/2025/07/decoded-key-operated-locking-devices/ | CONFIDENCE: medium (secondary summary of ICC text)
- FACT: NFPA 101 (2021 and later) lets key-operated locks be used on exterior doors and on doors to an individual or single tenant space only if: the door stays unlocked while the building or space is occupied; a key is immediately available to any occupant inside while it is locked; the lock is readily distinguishable as locked; a sign with 1-inch letters reads "THIS DOOR TO REMAIN UNLOCKED WHEN THIS SPACE IS OCCUPIED" (or "...WHEN THE BUILDING IS OCCUPIED"); and the AHJ may revoke this for cause. The same article cites the 2021 edition section as 7.2.1.5.6. | SOURCE: iDigHardware, "Decoded: Key-Operated Locking Devices" (July 2025); "Decoded: Key-Operated Locks" | URL: https://idighardware.com/2025/07/decoded-key-operated-locking-devices/ | CONFIDENCE: medium
- FACT: The same article says an egress-side thumbturn deadbolt is not subject to the key-operated lock rules. It must still meet other requirements, such as being the only locking device unless two releasing motions are allowed, and being mounted 34 to 48 inches above the floor. Local codes vary, and the AHJ has the final say. | SOURCE: iDigHardware, "Decoded: Key-Operated Locking Devices" (July 2025) | URL: https://idighardware.com/2025/07/decoded-key-operated-locking-devices/ | CONFIDENCE: medium

## 5. Hiring a locksmith: FTC, ALOA, state guidance, licensing

- FACT: The FTC press release "FTC Urges Consumers to Use Caution When Seeking a Locksmith" (May 30, 2008) advises researching a locksmith before you need one and saving the number in your phone. It warns that "some locksmiths advertising in your local phone book may not be local at all" and that others "may not have professional training." The FTC's fuller consumer article ("Finding a Locksmith") is no longer live on consumer.ftc.gov. | SOURCE: FTC press release | URL: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2008/05/ftc-urges-consumers-use-caution-when-seeking-locksmith | CONFIDENCE: high (but dated 2008)
- FACT: Wisconsin DATCP fact sheet (credits an FTC Feb 2008 fact sheet as a source). Tips: ask for the company's legal name if the phone is answered with a generic phrase like "locksmith services"; get an estimate for all work and parts before work starts, preferably in writing (for lockouts, legitimate locksmiths usually quote the total by phone); ask about extra charges (after-hours, mileage, service-call minimums); ask whether they are insured; ask for ID and a business card; check that the invoice, business card, and vehicle names match; get an itemized invoice. | SOURCE: Wisconsin DATCP, "Consumer Protection Fact Sheet - Hiring a Locksmith" | URL: https://datcp.wi.gov/Pages/Publications/HiringALocksmith470.aspx | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: Wisconsin DATCP warning signs: street addresses listed only to look local; "multiple discounts, emergency service promotions, claims of lowest prices, 'No. 1' self-ratings"; being told up front in a lockout that the lock must be drilled and replaced; the price changing after arrival; refusing to give a legal name; asking you to sign a blank form or pay in advance. | SOURCE: Wisconsin DATCP, "Hiring a Locksmith" | URL: https://datcp.wi.gov/Pages/Publications/HiringALocksmith470.aspx | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: ALOA warning signs: a "too-good-to-be-true" low price that rises after arrival; scammers "invest heavily in search engine ads"; a phone number that goes to an out-of-state location despite claims of local service; excuses for not picking a lock ("If they immediately go to drilling, this is often a sign of a scammer"); cash only. ALOA's checks: confirm a local address and phone; confirm "the name of the company you called matches the name of the company that arrives"; look for a uniform, ID, and a marked vehicle; where licensing applies, ask for the state-issued license; search FindALocksmith.com. Report problems to your state consumer protection office or attorney general. | SOURCE: ALOA, "How to Spot a Scammer" | URL: https://www.aloa.org/consumers/howtospotascammer | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: California requires locksmiths to be licensed by the Bureau of Security and Investigative Services (BSIS), and locksmiths must carry a pocket copy of their license. Consumers can verify at bsis.ca.gov or (800) 952-5210. Work over $500 at one site also requires a CSLB contractor's license. | SOURCE: California Dept. of Consumer Affairs, "Consumer Tips for Hiring a Locksmith" | URL: https://dca.ca.gov/publications/locksmith_tips.html | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: ALOA lists 13 states requiring locksmith licensing as of January 2025: Alabama, California, Connecticut, Illinois, Louisiana, Maryland, Nevada, New Jersey, North Carolina, Oklahoma, Oregon, Texas, Virginia. Local licensing applies in New York City and Nassau County, NY. Broward County, FL ended its requirement. Illinois licensing is scheduled to sunset January 1, 2029. THIS LIST MAY CHANGE; re-verify before publishing. | SOURCE: ALOA, Advocacy page | URL: https://aloa.org/advocacy | CONFIDENCE: high (as of Jan 2025)
- FACT: Locksmith Ledger (Aug 1, 2024) gives the same 13 states. It adds that Nebraska and Tennessee repealed locksmith licensing in 2021, that Nevada's requirement is a county-sheriff permit, that local licensing exists in NYC, Nassau County, Miami-Dade, and Hillsborough County (FL), and that Pennsylvania has no state locksmith license, though some locksmiths may need a Home Improvement Contractor registration. | SOURCE: Locksmith Ledger, "Locksmith Licensing: 2024 Update" | URL: https://www.locksmithledger.com/home/article/55128047/locksmith-licensing-2024-update | CONFIDENCE: medium

## 7. Smart deadbolts: protocols, alignment, batteries

- FACT: Matter 1.0 was released by the Connectivity Standards Alliance on Oct 4, 2022. It runs over Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and Thread and uses Bluetooth Low Energy for device commissioning. Door locks were among the first supported device types. | SOURCE: CSA, "Matter Arrives..." press release | URL: https://csa-iot.org/newsroom/matter-arrives/ | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: CSA's Matter FAQ: "Matter natively supports Wi-Fi, Ethernet, and Thread." Bluetooth LE is "commissioning only." "Thread is best suited for low-power devices (sensors, locks, switches)." "Matter is a local connectivity technology," and remote control from outside the home generally needs an internet-connected controller in the home. Multi-admin lets users choose which platforms control a device. "There is no native interoperability between Matter and Zigbee devices," though bridges can connect them. | SOURCE: CSA, Matter FAQs | URL: https://csa-iot.org/all-solutions/matter/matter-faq/ | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: CSA says Zigbee uses a "self-healing mesh network," is "optimized for low power consumption," and its PHY supports both 2.4 GHz and sub-GHz bands. | SOURCE: CSA, Zigbee page | URL: https://csa-iot.org/all-solutions/zigbee/ | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: Z-Wave Alliance: in the US, standard Z-Wave uses 908.42/916 MHz and Z-Wave Long Range uses 912/920 MHz. Standard Z-Wave is a mesh network (nodes can act as repeaters), covers about 100 m per hop, and supports 232 nodes. Z-Wave LR uses a star topology, supports up to 4,000 nodes, and is US-only for now. Security classes: S0, S2 non-auth, S2 auth, S2 Access (LR: S2 only). | SOURCE: Z-Wave Alliance, "What is Z-Wave" | URL: https://z-wavealliance.org/what-is-z-wave/ | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: TechTarget (Aug 29, 2018) says the Z-Wave Alliance has required certified devices to use the S2 security framework since April 2017. | SOURCE: TechTarget, "Z-Wave" definition | URL: https://www.techtarget.com/iotagenda/definition/Z-Wave | CONFIDENCE: medium
- FACT: Schlage Encode Plus: built-in Wi-Fi (needs a 2.4 GHz network), works with Apple Home/Home Keys, Alexa, and Google Assistant; four AA alkaline batteries, "up to six months of battery life with typical use"; one backup key (5-pin, C keyway); A156.40-2015 Grade AAA and A156.36 Grade 1; built-in alarm. | SOURCE: Schlage, Encode Plus (BE499WB CEN) | URL: https://www.schlage.com/en/home/products/BE499WBCENFFF.html | CONFIDENCE: high
- FACT: Schlage defines "Lock Jammed" as the deadbolt "encountering an obstruction either while extending into the strike plate or retracting back into the lock." It calls misalignment "a common cause of lock jamming": make sure the deadbolt and strike plate line up and the door is prepared to the lock's specifications. | SOURCE: Schlage support, "Understanding Lock Jammed in the Schlage Home App History" | URL: https://schlage-res.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/34505427253780-Understanding-Lock-Jammed-in-the-Schlage-Home-App-History | CONFIDENCE: high

## 11. Commercial deadbolts, ADA operable parts, access control

- FACT: Schlage: "Mortise locks are the most secure type of lock." ## Unverified / do not use

- UL 437 attack times (for example, "10 minutes picking, 5 minutes drilling/forcing"). These are widely repeated on LockWiki, forums, and blogs, and sources disagree (some say 10 minutes for drilling). The UL standard is paywalled, and no publisher or manufacturer page with the numbers could be read. Do not state specific times.
- UL 437 "1,000 key differs" and similar construction requirements: secondary only, not verified.
- Grade 2 and Grade 3 cycle counts for A156.36 deadbolts, and Grade B/C numbers for A156.40. BHMA's public summaries give only the top grade. Third-party guides often quote 1,000,000 / 500,000 / 250,000 (or 800,000/250,000 etc.) for "deadbolts," but those figures belong to other standards. Do not use them.
- "Grade 1 is the residential top tier": this mixes scales. A156.40 uses A/B/C (AAA = best in all three categories). Grade 1/2/3 is A156.36 (auxiliary) or A156.2/A156.13.
