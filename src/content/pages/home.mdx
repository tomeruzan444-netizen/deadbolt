## Why hire a deadbolt locksmith instead of a generalist

A deadbolt is the lock that actually resists force on a door. The knob latch below it holds the door closed. The deadbolt is what stops it from being forced open. That is why the details of a deadbolt matter more than any other lock you own, and why Deadbolt Fixers chose to be a deadbolt locksmith rather than a little bit of everything.

Focus shows up in small, practical ways:

- **We look past the lock.** Most "broken" deadbolts we see are working fine. The door has sagged, the strike has shifted, or the bolt is rubbing the frame. Adjusting the door often fixes it for a fraction of the cost of a new lock.
- **We check the whole system.** A strong lock is only as good as its strike. We look at strike screw length, the frame behind it and whether the bolt reaches its full throw.
- **We match hardware to the door.** Backset, door thickness, nearby glass and local egress rules all decide which deadbolt is right. Getting one wrong means new holes in your door.
- **We do not oversell.** If rekeying solves the problem, we will not sell you new locks. If a cheap lock is the weak point, we explain exactly why.

## What is inside the deadbolt on your door

A standard single cylinder deadbolt has six main parts. Knowing them makes it easier to describe a problem and to understand a quote.

<DeadboltExploded tone="dark" id="home-anatomy" labelled />

1. **Key**: lifts the pins inside the cylinder to the right height so the plug can turn.
2. **Exterior cylinder**: holds the pin stacks. Rekeying changes the pins here without replacing the lock.
3. **Bolt assembly**: the bolt itself, its housing and the faceplate on the door edge. Most residential bolts are made to extend 1 inch, and the backset is usually 2-3/8 or 2-3/4 inches.
4. **Interior mounting plate**: anchors the inside half, held to the outside half by two through-bolts.
5. **Thumb turn**: operates the bolt from inside without a key. A double cylinder lock has a second keyed cylinder here instead.
6. **Strike plate and screws**: the plate on the frame that receives the bolt. Screws long enough to reach the wall framing are what make a kick-in hard.

When any deadbolt locksmith diagnoses a problem, these are the parts being tested in order: does the key turn the plug, does the tailpiece move the bolt, and does the bolt meet a strike that lines up and holds.
