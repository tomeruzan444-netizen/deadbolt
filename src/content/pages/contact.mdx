## What to include when you schedule deadbolt service

A good request answers four questions. With these, a technician can arrive with the right parts instead of diagnosing on the doorstep and coming back.

1. **Where is the door?** City, state and ZIP code, and whether it is a house, apartment or business.
2. **What kind of door is it?** Wood, steel, fiberglass, or a door with glass near the lock.
3. **What is the lock?** The brand if you can see it, and whether it is keyed, keypad or smart.
4. **What is happening?** For example "bolt only goes in halfway", "key turns but nothing moves" or "we just moved in."

Not sure which job you need? The [list of every deadbolt service](/services/) matches common symptoms to the right visit.

## When to call instead of writing

Some problems should not wait for an email. If you are locked out, a key has broken off in the lock, or the door cannot be secured for the night, use a phone number if one is listed on this page. Our pages on [lockout service](/deadbolt-lockout-service/) and [removing a broken key](/broken-key-in-deadbolt/) explain what to do while you wait.

## Before you schedule deadbolt service, a quick self-check

Two minutes of checking can save a visit. If the bolt turns easily with the door open but drags with the door closed, the problem is almost always alignment, not the lock. Our guide to [fixing a deadbolt that won't lock](/deadbolt-guides/deadbolt-wont-lock/) walks you through the test. If you still want a technician afterwards, schedule deadbolt service with the form above and mention what you found, because it narrows the diagnosis.
