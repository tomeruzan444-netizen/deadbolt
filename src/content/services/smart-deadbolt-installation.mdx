## Why the door matters more than the app

Schlage's app has a log entry called "Lock Jammed." Schlage defines it as the bolt meeting an obstruction while it extends into the strike plate or retracts, and it names misalignment as a common cause. The lock is usually fine. The door is not lined up with it.

With a manual deadbolt, you never notice a slight rub. Your hand adds a little lift or a little push without thinking. A motor only has the force it was designed with, so if the bolt drags on the strike, it stops and reports a jam.

This is not new thinking. In an older guide, Schlage explained that it made one keypad deadbolt manual-throw because its engineers wanted the bolt to lock securely even as doors expand and contract with temperature and humidity.

So before any smart deadbolt installation, we fix the path the bolt travels:

- **Find the rub.** Schlage's method is to coat the bolt with chalk or lipstick, lock and unlock, and look for the mark on the strike.
- **Deepen the pocket.** Schlage and Yale both say the hole behind the strike should be at least 1" deep, so the bolt extends fully without dragging.
- **Move the strike.** Yale's advice when the bolt hits the plate is to reposition the plate to line up with the bolt.
- **Lift the door.** Schlage notes that worn or loose hinges let a door sag, and the fix is new hinges or shims.

If you want to work through those checks yourself first, our [guide to a deadbolt that won't lock](/deadbolt-guides/deadbolt-wont-lock/) covers them step by step.

## Choosing how the lock connects

Picking the radio is the first decision in any smart deadbolt installation, because it sets what else you need at home and how you control the lock from away. Here is how the common options compare, using what the standards bodies and manufacturers publish.

| Connection | How you reach it | Extra hardware | Worth knowing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keypad only | A code at the door | None | No app, no remote access |
| Bluetooth | Your phone, close to the door | A bridge or hub for remote use, depending on the model | Simple, short range |
| Wi-Fi | Your home router | None | Encode Plus, for one, needs 2.4 GHz |
| Z-Wave | A Z-Wave controller (hub or panel) | Yes | Mesh network, about 100 m per hop, S2 security classes |
| Zigbee | A Zigbee hub | Yes | Self-healing mesh, built for low power |
| Matter over Thread | A Matter controller in the home | Yes, for remote control | Multi-admin across platforms |

A few details matter more than the brochure suggests. Z-Wave in the US runs on its own frequencies (908.42 and 916 MHz for standard Z-Wave), away from Wi-Fi. A newer Z-Wave Long Range mode uses a star layout instead of a mesh and is US-only for now.

Matter is the newest option. The Connectivity Standards Alliance released Matter 1.0 on October 4, 2022, with door locks among the first device types. It runs over Wi-Fi, Thread and Ethernet, uses Bluetooth LE only to set devices up, and works locally, so remote control needs an internet-connected controller inside the home.

One trap: there is no native link between Matter and Zigbee. If your home already runs on a Zigbee hub, a Matter lock needs a bridge, and a Zigbee lock will not appear in a Matter app on its own.

## Retrofit or a full smart deadbolt installation

There are two ways to make a deadbolt smart, and they behave very differently on the door.

**A retrofit** replaces only the inside half of your existing deadbolt. The outside cylinder, the bolt and your keys stay. It is the least invasive choice, but it inherits everything about the lock underneath. Consumer Reports puts it plainly: a retrofit's kick, pick and drill resistance depends entirely on the deadbolt it is paired with. If that bolt already drags, the retrofit motor will drag too.

**A full smart deadbolt installation** replaces the whole lock. Most use the standard door prep, so they fit existing holes. The Schlage Encode Plus fits 1-3/8" to 1-3/4" doors at either common backset and carries A156.40 Grade AAA and A156.36 Grade 1 ratings, which a retrofit cannot add to a weak lock.

## Batteries, backup keys and dead-battery plans

Every smart deadbolt runs on batteries, and every one eventually warns you they are low. Schlage rates the Encode Plus at up to six months on four AA batteries with typical use. Yale's keypad lock signals low battery with a steady red light and five beeps, and Yale says to replace all four AA alkaline batteries together.

The mechanical key is your fallback when batteries or electronics fail. The Encode Plus ships with one backup key. Yale's support pages say directly that if the lock fails, you use the mechanical key.

<Callout type="tip">
A backup key in a kitchen drawer does nothing when you are outside with a dead lock. Keep one with someone nearby you trust, or somewhere you can reach from outside, and test it twice a year.
</Callout>

## What a smart lock does not change about security

Connectivity adds convenience, not strength. Consumer Reports found smart locks just as susceptible to forced entry as nonconnected locks. A kick still lands on the bolt, the strike and the frame.

That is why we check the strike screws during every smart deadbolt installation. If they only reach the jamb, [strike plate reinforcement with 3 inch screws](/deadbolt-strike-plate-reinforcement/) is worth doing while the door is already open.

On the digital side, the protocols build in their own protections. The Z-Wave Alliance lists S2 security classes, and TechTarget reports that certified Z-Wave devices have been required to use S2 since April 2017. Your part is housekeeping: delete codes and app users when someone moves out or a job ends.

## The one-finger test before you buy

Close your door and turn the existing thumb turn with one finger. If the bolt slides home and back with no push, pull or lift on the door, a motorized lock will be happy there.

If you need to lean on the door to lock it, the motor will feel the same resistance and lose. Get the alignment fixed first, and the smart lock you choose will work the way the box promises.
