## The lock is often not what's broken

When a deadbolt fights you, the natural guess is that the lock has failed. On most repair calls it hasn't. The bolt is rubbing a strike that has shifted, the door has dropped on its hinges, or years of grit and the wrong lubricant have built up in the cylinder.

That matters for your wallet. A deadbolt lock repair that corrects the strike or cleans the cylinder costs far less than new hardware, and it keeps a good lock on the door. Replacement only earns its place when the lock itself is worn, weak or damaged.

A useful benchmark comes from the residential deadbolt standard. BHMA's public summary of A156.40 says a Grade A lock must retract its bolt with no more than 9 inch-pounds of torque, which is a light turn. If yours takes a firm grip and a wrist twist, something is adding friction that the lock was never designed to carry.

## Symptoms we fix, and what usually causes them

The table below matches each complaint to its usual cause. Homeowners who want to try the basic checks themselves first can follow our [deadbolt won't lock troubleshooting guide](/deadbolt-guides/deadbolt-wont-lock/). This page covers what happens when those checks don't solve it.

| Symptom | Most likely cause | Typical fix on site |
|---|---|---|
| Bolt sticks or drags as it throws | Bolt rubbing the strike; door sag | Reposition or open up the strike; tighten or shim hinges |
| Grinding or scraping | Metal contact at the strike, or grit in the bolt housing | Correct the contact point; clean the mechanism |
| Bolt stops short of full extension | Strike pocket too shallow or off center | Deepen the pocket; realign the strike |
| Thumb turn wobbles or slips | Loose through-bolts or mounting plate; worn tailpiece | Tighten or replace fasteners; replace worn parts |
| Key hard to turn, getting worse over time | Worn cylinder pins, worn key, packed lubricant | Clean and relubricate, cut a key to code, or replace the cylinder |

### A bolt that sticks or drags

The quickest test is to throw the bolt with the door open. If it moves freely, the lock is fine and the problem lives in the strike or the door. Houses settle, hinge screws loosen and the bolt starts catching the top or bottom edge of the strike opening.

A Deadbolt Fixers technician finds the exact contact point, then moves or opens up the strike so the bolt clears it. If the door has sagged, the hinges get attention first, because moving the strike without fixing the sag only lasts until the next season.

### Grinding and scraping

Grinding is metal on metal, and it rarely fixes itself. On the strike side it wears a groove into the bolt and loosens the plate. Inside the lock, it usually means dirt in the bolt housing or a part that has started to wear.

### A bolt that won't fully extend

A residential deadbolt typically throws a 1-inch bolt, and it needs room to get there. Schlage's B-Series instructions call for a strike hole at least 1-1/4 inches deep. Builders and DIY installers sometimes drill shallower, which leaves the bolt short of full throw and easier to push back.

<Callout type="warning" title="Why a short throw matters">
A bolt that stops halfway can feel locked when it isn't fully engaged. If you have to force the last bit of travel, or the thumb turn springs back slightly, book the deadbolt lock repair rather than living with it.
</Callout>

### A loose or slipping thumb turn

The inside half of a deadbolt is held by two through-bolts that pass into the outside cylinder. When they loosen, the thumb turn wobbles and the tailpiece no longer engages the bolt cleanly. Sometimes tightening is enough. If the screw posts are stripped or the tailpiece is twisted, we replace those parts.

### A key that has become hard to turn

Gradual stiffness over months or years points to wear. Schlage's own troubleshooting guide says that when a key sticks or turns hard after the lock has been in use for a while, a replacement cylinder is required.

Before we replace anything, we rule out the key and the lubricant. Schlage's service manual names keys worn past factory specification, and copies made from copies, as common reasons a lock stops working well. It also warns that too much powdered graphite can pack the cylinder and stop the pins moving freely. A key that has actually snapped is a different job, covered on our [broken key extraction page](/broken-key-in-deadbolt/).

## How a deadbolt lock repair visit runs

Every deadbolt lock repair we do follows the same order, so nothing gets missed and you approve the cost before work starts.

1. **Isolate the fault.** We operate the bolt with the door open, then closed, then with the key from outside. That separates lock problems from door problems.
2. **Inspect the door.** Hinge screws, door sag, the strike position and the depth of the strike pocket.
3. **Inspect the lock.** Through-bolts, thumb turn, tailpiece, cylinder and the key you use every day.
4. **Quote the fix.** You get a price for the repair and, if it's close, the price of replacement alongside it.
5. **Repair and test.** We test every key you use, from both sides, with the door closed.

Smart locks get the same treatment. Schlage lists misalignment between bolt and strike as a common cause of the "lock jammed" alert in its app, so a motorized deadbolt that keeps reporting jams usually needs door work, not a new motor. For full retrofits, see [smart deadbolt installation](/smart-deadbolt-installation/).

## What moves the price of a repair

A deadbolt lock repair is quoted on what the fix involves, not a flat menu. These factors decide the number:

- **Door work versus lock work.** Strike and hinge adjustments need time but few parts. Cylinder or bolt replacement adds hardware.
- **Parts availability.** Common residential brands are carried on the van. Older or uncommon locks may need parts ordered.
- **Timing.** After-hours visits cost more. In Locksmith Ledger's 2024 reader survey, which it describes as not scientific, average labor was $104 per hour regular and $151 per hour after hours, with a $94 average service call. These are national averages and vary by location.
- **Other jobs at the same visit.** Rekeying or reinforcing while we're there shares the trip charge.
