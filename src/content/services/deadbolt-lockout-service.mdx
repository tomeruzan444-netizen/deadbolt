## First ten minutes: what to do while you're locked out

Stop and check every other way in before you call anyone. Back doors, garage entry doors and slider locks get left open more often than people think. Don't climb to a window or force a door, since a broken frame costs more than any lockout.

Then work through the people who might have a key: a partner, a neighbor, a family member, your landlord or building manager. If none of those work, it's time for a deadbolt lockout service.

<Callout type="warning" title="When to call 911 instead">
If a young child, a vulnerable adult or a pet is inside and at risk, or something dangerous like a stove or bath is running, call 911 first. Emergency services can act faster than any locksmith, and the lock can be dealt with afterward.
</Callout>

While you wait, find somewhere safe and visible to stand, keep your phone charged, and think about what proof of address you can get to. If your wallet is inside, tell us when you call. That changes how we verify, not whether we can help.

## Proof of residence: why we ask, and what counts

Opening a locked door is the one job where a locksmith can be used against the person who lives there. So we confirm two things before we start: who you are, and that you have the right to enter.

Here is what works, roughly in order of how quickly it settles things:

- **Photo ID showing the property address.** The fastest route.
- **ID plus a document with your name and the address.** A lease, a utility bill, a mortgage statement or a property tax record.
- **Confirmation from the owner or manager.** Common for tenants, house sitters and family members.

If your ID is inside, we can open the door and check your documents there with you before we leave. A deadbolt lockout service that skips this step entirely is one we would be wary of hiring.

## How we open a locked deadbolt without damage

Most residential deadbolts can be opened with specialist tools that manipulate the lock from the outside, the same way the right key would, and leave it working afterward. We don't publish the methods, but the principle is simple: work with the lock's mechanism, not against the door.

Drilling is a last resort. It is for a lock that has failed internally, or a [high security deadbolt](/high-security-deadbolt/) built specifically to resist manipulation. If it comes to that, we stop, explain why and quote the replacement cylinder before going ahead.

Some lockouts happen with the key in your hand. The key goes in but won't turn, or the bolt jams halfway. That is a worn or failing lock, and once you're inside we can quote a [repair for the sticking deadbolt](/deadbolt-lock-repair/) so it doesn't strand you again next week.

A key snapped off in the keyway is a different job with different tools. If that's what happened, see our page on [broken key extraction](/broken-key-in-deadbolt/), because forcing the door can push the fragment deeper.

## Keypad and smart deadbolt lockouts

Electronic deadbolts lock people out in their own ways: dead batteries, a forgotten code, a phone that won't connect. Most keypad and smart deadbolts keep a mechanical key cylinder as a backup, and Yale's support pages, for example, tell owners to use the mechanical key if the lock fails.

If that backup key is inside with everything else, a deadbolt lockout service opens the backup cylinder much like any keyed deadbolt. Once you're in, change the batteries, and if lockouts keep happening, talk to us about [setting up the smart lock properly](/smart-deadbolt-installation/), since Schlage names a misaligned bolt and strike as a common cause of smart lock jams.

## Lockout scam warning signs

Lockouts are a favorite target for bait-and-switch operators, because people who are locked out are in a hurry. The FTC warned back in 2008 that some locksmiths advertising in the local phone book "may not be local at all," and that others may lack professional training. State consumer agencies and the trade association ALOA list the same red flags today.

| Warning sign | Why it matters | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Phone answered with a generic phrase like "locksmith services" | Ask for the legal company name. A refusal is a red flag. | Wisconsin DATCP |
| Told on the phone the lock must be drilled and replaced | Most lockouts can be opened without drilling | Wisconsin DATCP; ALOA |
| A very low phone price that grows after arrival | Classic bait-and-switch | Wisconsin DATCP; ALOA |
| Asked to sign a blank form or pay in advance | You lose your leverage over the final bill | Wisconsin DATCP |
| Cash only | Hard to dispute and hard to trace | ALOA |
| Company name on the van, card and invoice don't match the one you called | You may be dealing with a lead-selling middleman | ALOA; Wisconsin DATCP |
| A "local" number that routes out of state | Often a call center dispatching subcontractors | ALOA |

Some states and cities license locksmiths. ALOA counted 13 states with licensing as of January 2025, and California, for example, requires locksmiths to carry a pocket copy of their state license. If something goes wrong, ALOA advises reporting it to your state consumer protection office or attorney general.

## What affects how soon our technician arrives

At Deadbolt Fixers, we don't promise arrival times we can't control, and you should be wary of anyone who guarantees one before knowing where you are. These are the things that decide it:

- **Distance and traffic** between our nearest available technician and your door.
- **Time of day and day of week.** Late nights and holidays mean fewer of our technicians are on call.
- **Demand.** Storms and cold snaps bring a wave of jammed and frozen locks at once.
- **The lock itself.** A high security cylinder or a smart lock may need one of our technicians with specific tools.
- **What you tell us.** An exact address, cross street and lock details help the right person arrive equipped.

After-hours calls also cost more. Locksmith Ledger's 2024 reader survey, which it describes as not scientific, put average after-hours labor at $151 per hour against $104 regular, with a $94 average service call. Local rates vary, and our [guide to deadbolt service costs](/deadbolt-guides/deadbolt-installation-cost/) explains how lockout quotes are built.

## Once you're back inside: one quick decision

Ask yourself one question: do you know exactly where your keys are? If they were left on the kitchen counter, you're done. If they were lost or stolen, especially with anything showing your address, book a [rekey of the deadbolt](/deadbolt-rekeying/) so the missing keys stop working. We can often do it during the same deadbolt lockout service visit, before the technician leaves.
